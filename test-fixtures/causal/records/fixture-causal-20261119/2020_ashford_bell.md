# Risk factors for the fixture outcome after fixture surgery: a prospective cohort

## Abstract

We studied which patient factors predict the fixture outcome within 30 days
of elective fixture surgery. In a prospective cohort of 1,200 adults at three
fixture centres, diabetes, current and former smoking and a higher body mass
index were each independently associated with the fixture outcome.

## Methods

We enrolled consecutive adults undergoing elective fixture surgery at three
fixture centres and followed them for 30 days. Diabetes and smoking status
(never, former, current) were taken from the preoperative assessment; body
mass index was measured in kg/m²; physical activity was recorded in MET-hours
per week. All models were multivariable and adjusted for age, sex and the
other factors listed.

## Results

Of the 1,200 patients, 216 (18%) had diabetes. In all, 576 (48%) had never
smoked, 360 (30%) were former smokers and 264 (22%) were current smokers. The
fixture outcome occurred in 108 patients (9%) within 30 days.

In the multivariable model, diabetes was independently associated with the
fixture outcome (adjusted OR 2.1, 95% CI 1.4–3.2). Compared with never
smokers, current smokers had higher odds of the fixture outcome (adjusted OR
2.4, 95% CI 1.5–3.8). Former smokers also had higher odds (adjusted OR 1.4,
95% CI 1.1–1.8). Each 1 kg/m² increase in body mass index raised the odds of
the fixture outcome (adjusted OR 1.05 per kg/m², 95% CI 1.02–1.08).

A one-SD higher physical activity was associated with a lower body mass index
(standardized beta −0.34, 95% CI −0.42 to −0.26).

## Discussion

Diabetes, smoking and body mass index can be addressed before elective fixture
surgery, and physical activity may act on the outcome through body mass index.
